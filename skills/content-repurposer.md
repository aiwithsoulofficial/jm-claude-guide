# Content Repurposer

Turn ONE piece of content into a full week of platform-specific posts.

## How to use this skill

Paste this file into your Claude project as a custom instruction. Then paste any piece of content that worked well and ask:

- "Repurpose this into a week of content"
- "Turn this caption into posts for all my platforms"
- "I have this email - turn it into social posts"
- "Take this and give me LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, email, and a reel script"

## Example prompts

- "Repurpose this Instagram caption into LinkedIn, Facebook, email, and a reel script"
- "I wrote this blog post - give me a week of social posts from it"
- "Turn this email into 5 different social media posts"
- "I have this carousel text - repurpose it for all platforms"

---

## What Claude will produce

### 1. LinkedIn Post (1,200-1,500 characters)
- Open with a scroll-stopping hook
- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences each) - LinkedIn rewards white space
- Clear CTA at the end: question, invite a comment, or point to a link
- Maximum 3 relevant hashtags

### 2. Instagram Caption - Educational (under 2,200 characters)
- Hook as the first line (all people see before "more")
- Teach one clear thing - numbered tips or a simple framework
- End with "Save this for later" or a question to drive comments
- 5-8 hashtags in a separate paragraph

### 3. Instagram Caption - Personal (under 2,200 characters)
- Same topic from a personal angle: story, lesson learned, behind-the-scenes
- Voice note to a friend feel, not a broadcast
- End with vulnerability or a question that invites real replies

### 4. Email to List (150-250 words)
- 2 subject line options: short, curiosity-driven, no clickbait
- Open like writing to one person, not a list
- One core message. One CTA. No walls of text
- Sign off in the user's usual style

### 5. Short-Form Video Script (30-60 seconds spoken)
- Hook in the first 3 seconds
- 3-4 punchy points written to be SPOKEN, not read
- CTA: follow, comment, save, or link in bio

## Voice rules

- Match the user's tone exactly from their content and brand guide
- If they're casual, be casual. If they're direct, be direct
- Never sound corporate or generic
- Use their words and phrases, not marketing jargon
